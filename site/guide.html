Text
  1. 1

    Choose today or a past storm

    Choose Today or Past storms, then a storm and a run.

    Today shows the newest forecasts. Past storms are grouped by the highest signal HKO issued, No. 10 first. Pick a storm, then a run, named after the time its forecast started, in UTC.

    The buttons below switch between ECMWF's physics model (IFS) and its AI model (AIFS). Older years use ECMWF's archived forecasts (2008–2023) or Pangu-Weather (2018–2022).

  2. 2

    What the forecast says

    The big number is the signal most of the ~50 forecast versions reach.

    ECMWF runs each forecast about 50 times. The page calls each version a member. The big number is the highest signal that most members reach, with when it starts and ends in Hong Kong time.

    Below it you see how many of HKO's eight reference stations get gale, storm or hurricane winds. For a past storm, HKO's own record sits next to it.

  3. 3

    See when each signal is up

    Bars show when each signal starts and ends, in Hong Kong time.

    Each bar covers one signal, in Hong Kong time. The solid part runs from the members' middle start to their middle end. The pale part spans the earliest 10% to the latest 90%.

    The thin line is the deterministic run, ECMWF's single main forecast. For past storms, HKO's real signals run along the top.

  4. 4

    Every line is one member

    Each line is one forecast run, coloured by the signal it expects along the way.

    ECMWF runs each forecast 50 times from slightly different starts; each run is a member. Each line is one member's storm track, coloured by the signal it expects Hong Kong to have at that point: grey for No. 1, then No. 3, No. 8, No. 9 and No. 10, and back down as the storm leaves.

    The thick black dashed line is the deterministic run. For past storms, the black dashed line with dots is where the storm really went. The circles mark distances from Hong Kong.

    The buttons under the map zoom in and out (+ −), move it (← ↑ ↓ →) and bring back the starting view (↺).

  5. 5

    Watch all members move

    ▶ all members plays every storm forward, 12 hours a second. Try it: this is the real map.

    ▶ all members moves every member's storm forward together, 12 forecast hours a second. The large black dot is the deterministic run.

    You can watch the spread grow, and see whether it closes on Hong Kong.

  6. 6

    Follow one member

    Click a line to see when that member expects each signal, by colour.

    Click any line to see that member alone, and where its storm is at every hour.

    Its track changes colour where that member expects each signal: grey for No. 1, then No. 3, No. 8, No. 9 and No. 10, and back down as the storm leaves.

    The player under the map steps through the hours. The large ← Back to all members button, at the top left of the map, brings everyone back.

  7. 7

    Zoom in on the eight stations

    zoom to Hong Kong colours HKO's eight stations by expected wind.

    zoom to Hong Kong shows HKO's eight reference stations. Each dot is coloured by the station's expected wind, with its Beaufort force next to the name.

  8. 8

    Read each station

    The Station guide tab gives each station's chances and expected wind.

    The Station guide tab under the map lists each station's chance of gale (63 km/h), storm force (88 km/h) and hurricane force (118 km/h), with a rough expected wind.

    For past storms it adds what HKO recorded and the difference. Red means the forecast was too low, green too high.

  9. 9

    Check how good it is

    Against MET WARN's 26 forecasts for the storms of 2025 and 2026: this site closer 10 times, MET WARN 6, same call 9.

    MET WARN, an unofficial Hong Kong weather channel, posts how likely it thinks HKO is to raise a signal. Each of its 26 forecasts for the storms of 2025 and 2026 is set beside this site's chance from the newest run out before the post, and what HKO did. If this site's chance is inside MET WARN's range, it is the same call; if not, the side nearer to what HKO did is closer. These storms were not used to set up this site.

    By signal: No. 3, this site 6, MET WARN 4, same 3; No. 8 and 9, 4, 1, 5; No. 10, 0, 1, 1. 1 not compared (No. 9, which this site does not forecast).
