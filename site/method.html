Method

How the forecasts on this site are made. Each section starts with the short version; open Full details for everything. Words are explained on Inputs by year.

  1. Forecasts. ECMWF runs each forecast about 50 times with small changes (the members). The site uses its physics model (IFS) and its AI model (AIFS); past cases also use older ECMWF forecasts (2008–2023) and Pangu-Weather (2018–2022).
  2. Strength. For every member, our strength model estimates the storm's strength every 6 hours.
  3. Wind reach. From that strength and the forecast's own winds: how far strong, gale, storm and hurricane winds reach toward Hong Kong (the rings).
  4. Signal chances. Hour by hour, each member's chance of No. 3, No. 8 and No. 10, from how close the storm comes, how strong it is and whether Hong Kong is inside its rings.
  5. The answer. The highest signal most members reach, when it starts and ends, and how many of HKO's 8 reference stations get gale, storm or hurricane winds.

1. Where the forecasts come from

  • Pick the year, the storm and the run, then one of the forecasts that exist for that run (the Forecast buttons); each forecast is its own system, judged on its own past.
  • AI forecasts: ECMWF's AI model (AIFS). Past cases 2018–2022 use Pangu-Weather, run afterwards — not a forecast anyone had at the time.
  • Physics forecasts: ECMWF's physics model (IFS). Past cases 2008–2023 use ECMWF's forecasts of the time (TIGGE archive), where the strength is estimated from ECMWF's storm list (Inputs by year).
  • Past cases are re-run with today's site; storms of 2025 and 2026 were not used to set it up, so they are a fair test, while older storms flatter it.
  • New runs come every 6 hours, about 13 hours after their start time.
  • Method codes on each run: storm strength S1 (from the forecast's weather maps), S2 / S3 (from ECMWF's storm list, older forecasts / 2024 versions); gale-wind reach R1 (the storm's own reach toward Hong Kong) or R2 (a typical reach for the strength) — see Inputs by year.
Full details
  • AI forecasts — AI weather models; strength S1.
  • Physics forecasts — ECMWF's physics model (IFS), the baseline. Strength S1 on the IFS forecast.
  • ECMWF ensemble of the time (TIGGE archive: 50 members + the control as member 51; the deterministic run shown apart). TIGGE keeps only the tracks, so the strength is S2, estimated from ECMWF's pressure and wind (against HKO's record on the site's 39 old storms it runs 18 kt too strong for depressions, 13 for tropical storms and 7 for severe tropical storms, about right for typhoons and 7-13 kt too weak for severe and super typhoons; Inputs by year), every step counted as a tropical cyclone; rings R1, toward Hong Kong from the storm's own 34 kt radius measured on the TIGGE 10 m winds (section 7) — provisional.
  • Pangu-Weather re-forecast (WeatherBench 2, run afterwards from ERA5 — not a forecast anyone had at the time). ONE run, so member counts read 0 or 1. Positions from our tracker started at ECMWF's analysis positions (storms that form inside the forecast are missing); strength S1 at every step; rings R2, a typical reach for the strength (no wind radii).
  • Past cases are re-run through today's pipeline and models. Storms of 2025 and 2026 were not used to set up the site, so a past case from those years is a fair test; older storms were, so their numbers flatter it.
  • Runs arrive every 6 hours, about 13 hours after their start time; the page says when its copy is behind. ECMWF takes its IFS ensemble to 144 h at 06 and 18 Z, so those cycles carry AIFS only.

2. Storm tracks and strength

  • The strength is worked out at every 6-hourly point of every storm within 1,600 km of Hong Kong.
  • Each member is drawn on the storm that gives it its highest signal; a dashed line is another storm the same member brings within 800 km.
  • Track colour: the highest signal that member gives, or its intensity class (colour by intensity).
Full details

Post-processed intensity at every 6-hourly step of every storm within 1,600 km of Hong Kong; a step the screen judges not a tropical cyclone keeps its raw field maximum.

Which storm a member is drawn on. Every member is drawn on the storm that gives it its highest signal (otherwise the storm the run starts with); a dashed line is another storm the same member brings within 800 km of Hong Kong, which still counts toward its chances. Colour is the highest signal that member gives a chance of 0.30 or more: No. 3, No. 8, pink = near No. 10 (below), No. 10, grey for none; thicker = a higher chance; the most threatening member is drawn thicker.

Bands on the intensity chart are HKO classes. Wind bands follow the wind categories: gale 34-47 kt, storm force 48-63, hurricane force 64 and above. Days are UTC.

3. The chance of each signal

  • Hour by hour, each member gets a chance of No. 3, No. 8 and No. 10 from how close the storm comes, how strong it is and how deep Hong Kong sits in its wind rings.
  • A centre crossing Hong Kong at 34 kt or more makes No. 8 certain, at 64 kt or more No. 10.
  • Every chance is also worked out with the track shifted up to 50 km and the strength by 5 kt: the small grey ranges.
  • Pink = near No. 10: not expected, but such a shift makes it plausible.
Full details

Direct hits. Whenever a track's centre crosses Hong Kong's territory at tropical-storm strength (34 kt) or more, No. 8 is taken as certain, and at typhoon strength (64 kt) No. 10 is. On record, all 9 storms that crossed at 34-63 kt drew No. 8 or higher, and 2 of the 3 that crossed at 64 kt or more drew No. 10 (Dujuan 2003 drew No. 9). Each shifted or weakened version of the track is judged on its own, so the range still shows when a direct hit is fragile.

No. 3 and No. 8 probabilities are hourly, on the track interpolated between its 6-hourly points (on storms it had not seen, the No. 3 chance finds 86% of past No. 3 storms with 13% false alarms), No. 10 hourly and gated at hurricane force, and each is bracketed over track shifts of up to 50 km and intensity shifts of 5 kt.

No. 10 tier: P(No. 10) is at or above 0.4 without any nudge — every No. 10 on record bar one was preceded by a No. 9, so expect a No. 9 first. Near No. 10 (pink): P(No. 10) is below 0.4, but shifting the whole track east or west by up to 50 km or the intensity by 5 kt lifts it to 0.4 or more — No. 10 is plausible, not expected. It is not a No. 9 forecast: over 159 past ensemble runs the share of pink members was about the same whether HKO stopped at No. 9 (8.6%) or went on to No. 10 (8.8%).

4. The signal sequence of one member

  • A signal is shown when this member's chance is at least 0.30 (No. 10: 0.40).
  • It steps down the way HKO's signals do (No. 10 → No. 8 → No. 3), with No. 9 in the 3 hours before No. 10.
  • On past cases, HKO's actual signals are the black dashed steps.
Full details

A signal is shown when this member gives it a chance of at least 0.30 (No. 10: 0.40; the same test used to count members for the majority); No. 1 while the centre is within 800 km; No. 9 is always shown in the 3 hours before No. 10, because HKO preceded 18 of its 19 No. 10s with a No. 9 (lasting a median 1.8 h). Signals step down the ladder as HKO's always do: No. 10 to No. 8, No. 8 to No. 3 (held at least 6 hours, HKO's median 6.3 h), never No. 8 straight to No. 1. Once a signal has been lowered it is raised again only if this member's chance of the higher signal is above 0.8 (HKO re-raised a signal for only 7 of 483 storms, e.g. Pabuk 2007: No. 1 → 3 → 1 → 3 → 8). On past cases the black dashed steps are what HKO actually issued.

5. Highest signal, start, end and hours

  • A member reaches a signal when its chance gets to 0.30 (No. 10: 0.40) at any hour; the headline is the highest signal most members reach.
  • Start, end and hours: the middle over those members, with the 10–90% range below.
  • The timeline shows the same on Hong Kong time; a second storm later in the run gets its own bar.
Full details

For each level (No. 3, No. 8, No. 10; No. 9 counts with No. 8) a member counts when its chance of that level or higher reaches 0.30 (No. 10: 0.40, the same threshold as the No. 10 tier) at any hour of the run; the headline is the highest level a majority of members reach. Small grey under the member count: the range over track shifts of up to 50 km and intensity shifts of 5 kt. For the members that reach a level: the median, and below it the 10–90% range, of the first hour, the last hour and the number of hours at that level or higher. A member's hours are its main episode: its hours at 0.30 or more, split where they pause for more than 12 hours, keeping the episode that holds its highest chance, so a second storm later in the run does not stretch the times. Times are Hong Kong time. The deterministic run is shown apart. For past cases, HKO's own record: when that signal or a higher one was first issued, when the last of them ended, and the hours in force. This replaces the day-by-day table, which split a signal at midnight, counted a two-day No. 8 twice and turned a few hours of timing error into a whole day.

Timeline. The same numbers on one Hong Kong-time axis, one row per level: the solid bar runs from the members' median start to their median end, the pale bar from the 10% earliest start to the 90% latest end, and a row is faded when fewer than half the members reach that level; the thin line is the deterministic run. On past cases the strip on top is HKO's signals exactly as issued (No. 1 to No. 10). When members' main episodes fall in separate periods -- a new period starts when an episode begins more than 24 hours after all earlier ones have ended, usually another storm (for example Mitag and Ragasa in the 17-18 Sep 2025 runs) -- each period gets its own bar with its own member count, start, end and hours; the answer card and the number cards give the largest period and list the others. The member count for the headline still counts every member that reaches the level, whichever period.

Other runs of this storm are in the Run menus: first the year (or the latest runs), then the storm, then the run; a run near two storms is listed under both. The map's dashed circles are 100, 200, 400 and 800 km from Hong Kong.

6. Winds at HKO's 8 reference stations

  • For each of HKO's 8 reference stations: the chance of gale (63 km/h), storm force (88) and hurricane force (118), from the storm's distance, strength and wind rings.
  • On the storms of 2025, a fair test, the gale count was exact for 9 of 12 storms and within one for 11.
  • The station table also gives a rough expected wind (km/h, with its Beaufort force); the map colours each station by it.
  • Several days ahead the count runs low for strong storms (gale about 1.7 stations low at 3–5 days): read the upper end of the range.
Full details

Severity is the number of Hong Kong's eight reference anemometers expected to reach gale force, the quantity the No. 3 and No. 8 criteria are written in. Since 28 Sep 2026 it is added up station by station: for each of the eight, the chance that it reaches gale, from how close the storm comes to that station, the storm's intensity then, how far each of its four wind rings (strong, gale, storm, hurricane) reaches past the station, whether the station lies on the storm's right or left (within 150 km of the centre the left side is not marked down: a storm that close is nearly as windy on both sides), and the hours the storm spends within 150 km of it; the eight chances are summed. No long-term trend is assumed (since 29 Sep 2026): the winds recorded for recent storms have been somewhat lower for their strength and distance, mostly for the storm as a whole rather than at particular stations (Tsing Yi and, less so, Kai Tak are the exceptions), and projecting that decline forward is not justified, so the model treats every year alike. On 2002-2024 this counts recent storms slightly high on average (about a quarter of a station). Checked season by season 2002-2024, each as a fair test, it gets the count exactly for 57% of storms and within one station for 93% (No. 8 or higher: 29% and 80%); on the 12 storms of 2025, a fair test, exactly for 9 and within one for 11. Cheung Chau sometimes reaches gale in a distant No. 3 storm in a way the track does not show, so weak storms often get a few tenths of a station; storms with four or more gale stations still come out about 1.1 stations too low. On the forecast page the rings are the site's rings: toward Hong Kong on the ECMWF, AI and old-storm pages (since 7 Oct 2026), the same all round on the Pangu pages. The quoted range (e.g. "3 (1–5)") is the measured spread for four storms in five at that distance, and the outcome percentages ("past storms at 3: No. 8 50%, …") are what past storms the model scored the same way actually drew, so they already carry its error. Nothing is shown for members beyond 722 km, the range it covers, and a member outside the range of past storms is flagged as such (⚠ on the forecast page).

Storm and hurricane force. The same station-by-station approach gives the number of reference stations at storm force and at hurricane force. Storm force means 88 km/h or more, hurricane force 118 km/h or more. At every station the chance of storm force never exceeds that of gale, and hurricane never exceeds storm. Checked as a fair test, the storm-force count is off by 0.22 stations on average and the hurricane-force count by 0.04; storms that did bring storm force still come out about half a station too low. The HKO card's counts come from the same values.

Stations — rough wind guide (forecast page, since 30 Sep 2026). For each reference station, the median chance of gale over the members and a rough 10-minute wind read back from the three chances: a chance near a threshold reads as a speed (e.g. a gale chance of 0.5 is about 63 km/h, 0.25 about 58); otherwise only a range can be given (below 53, 73–78, 98–108 km/h). Hurricane force is the top level, so nothing above it can be read from the chances. As storm force becomes likely at a station, the guide is lifted gradually toward a second, simple model — the open-sea wind at the station from the storm's own rings (22, 34, 48 and 64 kt and the peak near the centre), times the share of it the station usually gets (Cheung Chau 1.08, Chek Lap Kok 0.81, Sai Kung 0.76, Lau Fau Shan 0.74, Kai Tak 0.64, Tsing Yi 0.54, Ta Kwu Ling 0.46, Sha Tin 0.44), with its usual spread (0.70 to 1.12 times). A station the centre passes within the radius of maximum wind gets the storm's peak: it crosses the eyewall on the way in or out. The weight given to it rises with the storm-force chance, from nothing at 50% to all of it at 100% (the site owner's idea), and it only ever raises the guide. In fair tests, this beat an earlier hard switch at a 30% hurricane chance (error on stations that recorded 100 km/h or more 19.7 against 22.9 km/h, 2002-2024; 16.0 against 17.2 in 2025), removed the jump at the switch, and never showed 118 or more where less than 100 was recorded. It scales with the storm, so a very strong storm close by gives a number well above 118 — the wind the track implies. Real storms can fall short of it: in fair tests it gave Mangkhut 2018 at Cheung Chau about 152 km/h (recorded 173), Vicente 2012 about 125 (140), Hato 2017 about 173 (133) and Saola 2023 about 177 (126). A storm whose centre passes right through the stations (Dujuan 2003) can still be badly under-called: its one hurricane-force station, Lau Fau Shan (120 km/h), had never reached hurricane force before, so the chance of it stays low. On the forecast page the rings are the site's rings: toward Hong Kong on the ECMWF, AI and old-storm pages (since 7 Oct 2026), the same all round on the Pangu pages. It is guidance, not a wind forecast: there is no separate wind-speed model (one was tried on 30 Sep and did worse). For past cases the table also shows the highest 10-minute mean each station recorded, and the difference (expected minus recorded, as shown: red when the expected wind is below what HKO recorded, green when above, plain when equal; a band such as <53 is 'in range' when HKO's value lies inside it, otherwise the difference to its edge, or to its middle for 73–78 and 98–108).

Stations on the map (since 30 Sep 2026). Zoomed in to Hong Kong, the map shows the eight reference stations, each coloured by its expected wind in the station table for the same member (since 8 Oct 2026: the member clicked, or else the run the charts show — the deterministic run, the AI control run or the most threatening member): grey below 53 km/h, green 53–62 (strong; 41–52 cannot be told from the chances, so it stays grey), blue gale 63–87, orange storm force 88–117, red hurricane force 118 or more, with HKO's Beaufort force of that wind beside each name (e.g. F10; ≤F7 below 53 km/h); hovering shows the expected wind and the three chances. The map shows one member while the counts on the answer card are medians over all members, so the two can differ.

What this says. The live ensembles are well judged: HKO's gale count fell inside the shown range in about 80-95% of runs, as a 10–90% range should, so the ranges are not widened. Days ahead the median runs low for strong storms (gale about 1.7 stations low at 3–5 days, against 0.3 off within a day) and the strongest station winds come out about 18–28 km/h low 1–5 days ahead: many members pass wide of Hong Kong or weaker, and the median follows them — for a strong storm several days out, read the upper end of the range. The archived reconstructions (TIGGE, with an estimated strength) do worse, HKO inside the gale range about two times in three. By station the guide leans low at the exposed stations and high at the sheltered ones (live ensembles: Sai Kung -9.2, Cheung Chau -7.7, Lau Fau Shan -6.5, Chek Lap Kok -2.3, Ta Kwu Ling +2.6, Kai Tak +6.6, Sha Tin +11.5, Tsing Yi +12.3 km/h). A widening of the ranges was checked on 2023-2026 before any of this was known and rejected: it made the live ensembles' ranges too wide.

On the answer card. Eight dots, one per reference anemometer: filled up to the median over the members of each member's estimate rounded to whole stations, grey up to the members' 90th percentile, with the number beside them and the 10–90% range and the deterministic run's value after it. A member with no storm within 722 km counts as 0 there. On past cases the HKO card gives the count HKO recorded (annual reports).

When it is taken. Each station's chance uses the hour the storm is closest to that station. The moment shown beside the count and the gale-ring figure ("reaches N km past Hong Kong" / "stops N km short") are taken when this member's wind index at Hong Kong peaks; when that is not its closest approach (a storm that comes closest only after weakening or passing) the page gives that moment.

Hurricane-force winds. The page compares how far hurricane-force winds extend from the centre with the closest approach: if the ring reaches the closest approach they would cover Hong Kong, otherwise they stay offshore; no hurricane ring near Hong Kong means the No. 10 criterion is not met. Past storms reaching No. 10 carried about 83 km at closest approach, those stopping at No. 9 about 42 km.

7. How far the winds reach (the rings)

  • Four rings: how far strong (22 kt), gale (34), storm (48) and hurricane (64) winds reach on the side facing Hong Kong.
  • R1: worked out from the storm's own wind reach in the forecast and put on HKO's scale; the Pangu pages use R2, a typical reach for the strength.
  • The same rings drive the chances: inside a ring, the chance cannot fall far below how often HKO had that signal there (No. 8 in 71% of hours inside the gale ring).
Full details

The four rings — strong wind 22 kt, gale 34, storm 48, hurricane 64 — are the storm's own radii put on the Hong Kong Observatory's scale, toward Hong Kong (next paragraph). The Pangu pages, which have no radii, use a typical size for the intensity. A ring exists only when the post-processed intensity reaches its threshold. The SAME rings decide the signals: in HKO's own record, the deepest ring over Hong Kong matches the highest signal issued for 90% of storms since 2007 (gale ring → No. 8 in 22 of 25). Each member's probability is the larger of the statistical signal model and a model of how deep Hong Kong sits in its rings, and near or inside a drawn ring the chance cannot fall far below HKO's own record for that situation (inside the strong ring No. 3 or higher was in force 80% of the time, inside the gale ring No. 8 or higher 71%, inside the hurricane ring No. 10 74%): the full rate from 50 km inside the ring, half of it at the ring's edge, none from 50 km outside (since 7 Oct 2026; before, the full rate switched on at the edge) and when Hong Kong is more than 100 km outside a ring the chance is held to HKO's own rate at that distance (No. 8: 11% at 100-150 km, 4% at 150-200 km) — so the rings on the map and the chances stated cannot contradict each other in either direction. Radii are made consistent with the post-processed intensity, and move with it when the bracket shifts.

Rings toward Hong Kong (ECMWF, AI and old-storm pages, since 7 Oct 2026). HKO records every ring per quadrant, and what matters for Hong Kong is how far the winds reach toward it; the side facing Hong Kong is often the larger. Each ring is now the radius in the direction of Hong Kong (drawn as a circle of that radius): it is worked out from the storm's own 34 kt radius toward Hong Kong (ECMWF's quadrants; on the 2025 AI pages our tracker's quadrants; on the old-storm pages the radius measured on the TIGGE 10 m winds), the strength and the storm's position relative to Hong Kong (on 2025, a fair test, the median member's gale ring toward Hong Kong is 3 km too small), and the ring chances use how deep Hong Kong sits in its rings toward it. On HKO's own record this caught more No. 8 storms with no extra false alarm (per-storm CSI 0.79 → 0.84; 95% range of the gain +0.005 to +0.11); on the 55 runs of the 2025-26 test cases the forecasts stayed as good as before or a little better (Brier score No. 8 0.207 → 0.200, No. 10 0.175 → 0.174, the same No. 3 / No. 8 triggers, one extra No. 10 false alarm). On the 112 runs of the ECMWF ensemble of the time (TIGGE, 2008-2023) the storm's own radius did better than the typical rings (Brier score No. 8 0.315 → 0.284, three more No. 8 storms caught, one extra No. 10 false alarm). The Pangu past cases keep the four-quadrant typical rings: the ring toward Hong Kong did worse there.

The wind at Hong Kong (the dashed line on the intensity chart and the map panel) is a simple linear interpolation between the rings. A ring appears at its full size once the intensity reaches its threshold, as in HKO's best track (hurricane ring at 65 kt recorded in 94% of cases, median 56 km; at 60 kt in 4%).

8. The Hong Kong Wind Index

  • HKCOC's unofficial index of how windy Hong Kong is (km/h, on its own scale), estimated hour by hour from each member's wind field while the storm is within 800 km.
  • Checked on the storms as they really were: the storm peak is off by about 5 km/h; it runs low for the very strongest (Mangkhut 2018: 79 against 98).
Full details

The Hong Kong Wind Index is the unofficial index of the Hong Kong Tropical Cyclone Chasing Station (v4.hkcoc.com/windindex): a weighted average of the 10-minute winds and gusts at 20 HKO stations (8 urban, 12 offshore and high ground), in km/h, on its own scale: light below 14, fresh 14-24, strong 25-44, very strong 45-74, extreme 75 or more. Here it is estimated every hour from this member's own wind field — where Hong Kong sits in its rings, its intensity and position, the same field that sets the rings and the chances — while the storm is within 800 km. Checked on the storm as it really was (HKO's best track, not a forecast): the hourly estimate is off by 5 km/h and the storm peak by 5.0; on 80 older storms (2004-2021) the peak is off by 6.8 against the published values. It runs low for the very strongest: Mangkhut 2018 comes out at 79 against 98, Hato 2017 at 75 against 78. A forecast adds its own track, intensity and ring errors on top. How good is it?

Ensemble mean. The mean of the members' Hong Kong wind index (the deterministic run not counted). A member whose storm is farther than 800 km (the range the index covers), or has none, counts as 14 km/h: Hong Kong's observed index with a storm 700–800 km away (378 hours, 29 storms, 2022–2025). Members peak at different times, so the mean peaks lower than the strongest members do. The range quoted is 10–90% of members at the mean's peak.

9. Using the page

  • Click a track to see it below, then click anywhere on it, drag the time bar under the map or press play to see the storm and its wind rings move through time.
